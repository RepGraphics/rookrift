# Legal pages and account deletion

Release 0.12 adds public Terms of Service at `/terms`, Refund Policy at `/refund-policy` and Privacy Policy at `/privacy-policy`. They are linked from the landing page, authentication pages, public community footer and account settings. Dark/light appearance, section navigation and printing are supported. `/api/legal` exposes only the public operator fields and policy version.

The supplied operator is **Euphoria Development**, **United Kingdom**, with **support@euphoriadevelopment.uk** for support and privacy enquiries. No address has been invented. The pages show an operator-review notice while `LEGAL_BUSINESS_ADDRESS` is empty. Optional `LEGAL_OPERATOR_NAME`, `LEGAL_CONTACT_EMAIL`, `LEGAL_COUNTRY` and `LEGAL_BUSINESS_ADDRESS` environment settings override these public details. Compose passes them through its existing `env_file`; a Dokploy deployment can supply them as environment variables. These fields are public information, not secrets.

## Before commercial launch

These are product-specific drafts, not a legal compliance certification. Have the terms and privacy information reviewed for the actual operator, customers and deployment. Supply the required business contact address and check whether company, VAT or other registration information applies. Verify data processors, transfer safeguards, backup schedules and retention periods against the live service. Setting an address removes the missing-address notice; it does not perform a legal review.

The current refund offer allows a request within 14 calendar days of the first Rookrift membership payment. This is a proposed operator policy, additional to mandatory rights. It does not promise automatic pro-rata refunds for unused time or silently waive UK consumer cancellation rights when someone logs in. Requests go to the support address and are handled manually through the original payment provider. There is no automatic refund endpoint. Rookrift membership charges and community-merchant store purchases have separate policies and responsibilities.

Official references used for drafting: [UK online and distance selling guidance](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses), [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents), and [ICO information for the public](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/). The application does not yet record acceptance of a versioned contract or an express early-supply request; review checkout information and consumer-contract requirements before launch.

## Closing an account

Open **My account → Delete account**. The section is available to workspace staff as well as owners; it concerns their own identity. Support impersonation cannot perform deletion. Refresh the cancellation check after cancelling each owned workspace's paid membership. A subscription to someone else's workspace is not charged to the departing member.

The backend checks live Stripe subscription state across all owned workspaces, including subscriptions that local data says are cancelled. It accepts a provider-confirmed period-end cancellation; deletion then ends the remaining Rookrift access immediately. It does not cancel a renewing membership for the user. Pending Stripe subscription checkout links are expired during final deletion. Provider errors, nonterminal PayPal subscriptions, unresolved external checkout attempts and unverified manually managed paid memberships block closure. Resolve those with the provider/support and retry; the UI does not pretend external billing has been cancelled. Existing Stripe customers can open the billing portal even when new-checkout prices are not fully configured.

Deletion requires a fresh identity check using the current local password, a registered passkey with user verification, or the exact linked Discord identity. Verification expires after ten minutes and is bound to the current session. Accounts with an enabled authenticator also require its current code. The final request requires the literal `DELETE` and an explicit acknowledgment. CSRF checks apply to all deletion mutations. No real account is deleted by the browser demo: its simulation only clears browser demo records.

The account is fenced against new checkouts and game actions while closure runs. Active workflows must be cancelled and running configuration deployments, server commands or purchase actions must finish first. Store checkouts share buyer/merchant account locks with closure. Pending payment links are closed or require support resolution. A lost Stripe checkout response keeps a durable attempt marker; refresh the order to recover its provider reference or wait for its one-hour checkout expiry before retrying closure. Unsent purchase actions are cancelled. Commands already sent to a game and Discord roles already granted cannot be recalled by deleting an account. The service must remain a single runtime using its PostgreSQL lease; a startup clears an interrupted deletion fence after the transaction has rolled back.

## Data removed and retained

The transaction removes the real user, local login, passkeys, authenticator records, sessions, linked identities, community memberships, personal applications/RSVPs and staff access. It removes owned server connections and encrypted credentials, automations, configuration and operational history. Owned public community pages and stores close. Actual game-hosting processes, game saves and provider accounts remain with their provider.

Limited financial workspace shells, payment/customer references, purchased terms and transaction records remain for accounting, claims and reconciliation. They are closed, assigned to a non-login `Deleted member` identity and excluded from normal workspace/platform lists. Order recipients and pending delivery payloads are cleared. This preserves payment webhook reconciliation without restoring access or executing rewards. Other communities can retain justified moderation, audit, note and financial records; known author fields are anonymised, but free text can still contain historic names. Provider-held data is governed separately. Account closure does not issue refunds.

Privacy requests can be emailed while a billing issue is unresolved; the self-service billing safeguard does not override data-protection rights. Operators must apply the appropriate legal retention limits rather than retaining financial shells indefinitely by default. Backup copies expire on the deployment's schedule. Record erasure requests outside the removed account and reapply them before restoring a backup to service; never let a restore silently reactivate erased logins or closed communities. Review external processors and hosted backups as part of a specific erasure request.

## Deployment and verification

Migration 17 adds deletion proofs/challenges, an account-closing fence, closed workspace markers and durable pending membership checkout records. Back up PostgreSQL and retain the encryption key before upgrading. Use one application replica and stop-first replacement; do not run two schedulers against the same schema. No additional service or dependency is required.

Automated tests cover live cancellation checks, multiple workspaces, provider failure, CSRF, session-bound proof expiry, password/passkey/Discord verification, authenticator enforcement, support-view restrictions, checkout races, active work and retained/anonymised records. UI tests cover the public pages, links, cancellation gate, explicit confirmation and demo-only closure. Test real provider cancellation, OAuth and passkey UX against disposable test accounts before a paid launch. Never test deletion on the production owner's only account.
